1. Purpose and product direction

inFORMed is a modular legal assistance platform. It helps people understand and navigate legal processes, prepare the information their applications require, and generate the legal documents they need.

The first module helps people in South Africa prepare protection order applications. I am designing the platform so that more legal tools can be added later without rebuilding it for each new service.

This document is my reference while I develop inFORMed. It records how the platform is designed, how I am building it, what data it uses and where that data comes from, and the decisions and open questions that shape the work.

Core product principle
inFORMed combines structured legal workflows, verified legal information and document automation, with AI used where it adds value. AI assists the process. It is never the authority that determines a person's legal rights or makes legal decisions.

inFORMed helps people complete each step while they stay in control of the information they give and the documents they prepare. It does not invent facts, guarantee legal outcomes or present unverified AI interpretations as established law.

2. Platform architecture

The architecture separates the user experience, legal workflows, legal rules, legal knowledge, document generation and secure case information. Keeping these apart makes the platform easier to maintain, validate and extend to other legal processes.

AI services across the platform: natural-language understanding, plain-language explanations, translation, voice, and help organising what the applicant says. AI outputs always pass through the workflow rules, validation and the applicant's own review.

Each layer is a module within the inFORMed platform, sharing one codebase and one hosting environment.

2.1 User interface

The interface is clear, accessible and supportive. People often complete a legal application under great stress, so every question is easy to understand, the application is broken into manageable sections, and each step comes with a plain explanation. People can type or speak, listen to the replies, and hear their whole form read back before they sign it.

2.2 Workflow engine

The workflow engine manages the application process. It decides which questions are asked, in what order, and which further questions are needed based on the applicant's answers. It follows conditional pathways, so applicants are not all pushed through the same questionnaire.

2.3 Legal rules engine

This component applies approved legal and procedural rules. It works out which requirements apply, which information is needed, which exceptions are relevant and which path the workflow takes. Every rule is traceable to its legal source and approved by GRIT's legal advisor.

2.4 Legal knowledge service

This service gives the platform verified legal information: legislation, regulations, official forms and approved explanations. Every piece of information carries its source reference, and nothing in it can override an explicit workflow rule.

For the protection order module, the approved knowledge is small enough that I give it to the AI in full on every turn. This means nothing can be missed by a poor search. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), where the system first searches the approved knowledge for the relevant passages and gives only those to the AI, becomes necessary as the knowledge grows: when there are several legal modules, and when the platform includes a directory of courts, police stations and support services. When I add retrieval, it will use the same approach as Zuzi, so GRIT maintains one knowledge system. In both cases only approved content is used, and every answer can be traced to its source.

2.5 Document generation and review

The information collected through the workflow is mapped to the official templates. The system checks that the required fields are complete, generates the documents, and gives the applicant the chance to review and correct them before downloading or printing.

2.6 Secure data and audit layer

This layer protects personal case information, controls access and records important actions. It keeps individual case information completely separate from the platform's legal knowledge.

inFORMed keeps case information on the applicant's own device, so nothing about their case is held by GRIT. If GRIT later decides to store applications, for example so that a support worker can help review them, every record will be encrypted, stored in South Africa and deleted after an approved period, and every view, change and export will be logged with who did it and when.

3. Build: high-level overview

This section describes how I am building inFORMed: the technology, how the code is organised, how a conversation works, and how the platform is tested and deployed.

3.1 Shared core and legal modules

The code is split into a shared core and separate legal modules. The shared core contains everything every legal tool needs: the safety layer, languages, voice, channels, message limits and usage counters. Each legal module contains only what is specific to it: its workflow, legal knowledge, AI instructions and document templates. The protection order is the first module. When I add a second, it plugs into the same core and gets the same safety and language support without any of it being rebuilt.

3.3 Hosting and deployment

  • inFORMed runs on Google Cloud Run in Johannesburg (africa-south1), under GRIT's own Google Cloud organisation and billing account, next to Zuzi.
  • The OpenAI key and the WhatsApp keys are kept in Google Secret Manager. No key is ever written in the code or in the repository.
  • I deploy from the repository with a single command. Cloud Run builds the server, runs it, and gives it a secure web address.
  • The number of running copies is capped, which also caps cost, and a budget alert is set on GRIT's billing account and a monthly limit on the OpenAI account.
  • WhatsApp conversation state and message limits use a small Redis store. A WhatsApp conversation is kept for 24 hours only, and the applicant can delete it at any time by sending DELETE.
  • The prototype stays online at its current address until the new platform is ready to replace it.

3.4 AI models

All AI calls are made from the server, so the browser never sees a key. Each model is set in configuration, so I can change a model without changing code, and nothing is stored at OpenAI.

OpenAI is retiring the models the prototype used (gpt-4.1, gpt-4o-transcribe and gpt-4o-mini-tts) between 6 January and 26 February 2027, so I am moving to the models above before then. A new model only reaches users after it passes the golden and red-team tests.

3.5 How one conversation turn works

  1. The applicant sends a messageThe message, the form so far and the chosen language are sent to the server. Spoken messages are first turned into text, and the applicant checks what was heard before sending.
  2. Message limit checkStops one person sending hundreds of messages and running up costs.
  3. Safety check before the AIThe message is checked against GRIT's danger phrases. A match sends GRIT's approved emergency reply straight away, and the AI is not used.
  4. The AI answers in a fixed formatThe AI receives the module's instructions and approved knowledge, and must answer with a reply, the form updates, the types of abuse described, the sources it used and a safety label.
  5. Safety check after the AIIf the AI labels the message as an emergency, suicide risk, injury or risk of harm, its reply is replaced with GRIT's approved reply. Distress without immediate danger adds a fixed support line.
  6. The form is kept in EnglishWhen the conversation is not in English, every form update goes through a separate translation step, keeping names, dates and numbers exactly as given.
  7. The reply is shownThe applicant sees the reply, the form updates appear in the form panel, and tick lists are offered where the form has them.

3.6 Official form generation

inFORMed fills the Department of Justice's own fillable Form 6 and Form 6A (J480E, March 2025). It writes into the form's own boxes and ticks its official tick boxes, only from what the applicant has said. Text that does not fit a box continues on an annexure page. The applicant can download the official PDF, an editable Word draft, or print the form, together with a "Things to bring to court" checklist and an explanation of what happens after the form is completed.

3.7 Languages and voice

The conversation works in English, isiZulu and Afrikaans, and follows the applicant's own language. The form is always completed in English for the court. Applicants can speak instead of typing, listen to any reply, and hear their whole form read back in their language before they sign it. All page text in the three languages is held in one translation file, and every translation is checked by a native speaker.

3.8 Testing and quality

  • Automated checks: a set of checks runs after every change, without using real AI credit. It covers the danger replies, the support line, the English form, tick lists, document generation, voice and WhatsApp.
  • Golden tests: fixed conversations with the expected behaviour, run after every batch of changes and before any model change.
  • Red-team tests: deliberately difficult and risky conversations, in all three languages, to make sure the safety layer holds.
  • Legal and safeguarding review: the legal advisor checks the generated documents and explanations, and the safeguarding lead checks all safety wording.

3.9 Monitoring

inFORMed records content-free usage counters only: which tool was used, the language, the channel, the step reached, voice or typing, and the number of forms produced. No names, no stories and no messages are recorded. The counters feed a simple dashboard that shows where applicants stop and what to improve.

These categories have different owners and different rules. Legislation, official documents, procedures, rules and explanations are not personal information, so I collect them widely, and every item is checked for accuracy, kept current and approved by the legal advisor. Evaluation data is created only for testing and never contains a real person's case. Individual case information, described below, is the opposite: it is highly sensitive, and inFORMed uses only what the legal process requires.

4.1 Individual application and case information

The information an applicant enters is kept completely separate from the legal knowledge. For a protection order it includes:

  • Applicant and respondent details.
  • The relationship between the applicant and the respondent.
  • The applicant's description of incidents, including dates and places where known.
  • Information about children or dependants, where relevant.
  • The protection being requested.
  • Supporting information and evidence details.
  • Any other information the official forms require.
Data separation: Individual case information is not community knowledge. It is never added to the legal knowledge, never shared between cases and never used to train AI models. Its handling follows the privacy, security and legal requirements in section 10.

7. Protection order application workflow

The first legal module follows the actual legal process and the current official forms. The workflow below is my initial product model, and GRIT's legal advisor validates it before I implement it.

  1. Start applicationinFORMed explains what it is for and what it cannot do, gives the privacy information and asks for consent.
  2. Identify the applicantinFORMed establishes whether the person is applying for themselves or helping someone else, and adds any extra requirements for that path.
  3. Understand the circumstancesinFORMed gathers the relevant facts through carefully designed questions, and lets the applicant describe events in their own words.
  4. Identify the applicable legal pathwayinFORMed applies the approved legal rules to find the requirements that apply and any further questions.
  5. Identify the protection requestedinFORMed explains the options available under the law and lets the applicant choose what they are asking for.
  6. Collect supporting informationinFORMed gathers the remaining information the application needs and lists the supporting documents to bring.
  7. Generate the applicationinFORMed maps the validated answers to the official form and produces the documents.
  8. Review and exportThe applicant reviews and corrects the documents, then downloads or prints them, with clear information about the next steps.

7.1 How the workflow relates to the official form

The official application is Form 6, with the applicant's confidential personal details on Form 6A (court form J480E, March 2025). The workflow gathers the information for its ten sections:

Sections 5, 6 and 7 carry the most weight, because the magistrate decides mainly on what happened, why it is urgent and what the applicant is asking for. They are also where applicants most need support, because people rarely tell their story in the order a form expects.

inFORMed preserves the applicant's own account. AI helps organise the information into the right sections, but it never adds facts, strengthens allegations or turns uncertainty into certainty. The applicant can review and correct any wording the AI has helped with.

Where it matters, inFORMed distinguishes between what the applicant personally experienced, what someone else told them, what they believe happened and what they are unsure about.

10. Privacy, security and responsible data handling

Protection order applications contain identity information, allegations of abuse, health information, information about children and sometimes sensitive evidence. Privacy and security are therefore built into the architecture from the beginning, not added afterwards.

  • Data minimisation: inFORMed uses only the information the legal process requires, or a clearly justified product function.
  • Encryption: information is protected in transit and at rest.
  • Access control: only authorised people can access anything, based on their role and a legitimate need.
  • Data separation: legal knowledge, evaluation data and individual case information are kept apart.
  • Consent and transparency: applicants are told what information is used, why it is needed and how it is handled.
  • Retention and deletion: every kind of data has an approved retention period and a way to delete it.
  • Auditability: access, changes, exports and administrative actions are recorded wherever personal information is held.
  • AI processing controls: any transfer of sensitive information to an external AI service is assessed and approved before real applicants use inFORMed.
  • Training restrictions: identifiable case information is never used to train AI models.

I review the data protection requirements under POPIA and other relevant laws with GRIT's legal advisor and Information Officer before deployment.

10.1 What this means in practice

  • Special personal information: POPIA gives extra protection to certain information, including information about health and alleged criminal behaviour. Descriptions of abuse and injuries fall into this category, so inFORMed has a clear legal basis for processing them before it is used by real applicants.
  • Children's personal information: information about children affected by the abuse is also specially protected and handled with particular care.
  • External AI services: when an AI model hosted outside South Africa processes what an applicant writes, personal information crosses borders. I assess this before deployment, with an agreement in place with the provider that sets out how the information is handled and confirms it is not used to train their models.
  • Information Officer: GRIT's Information Officer is responsible for POPIA compliance and signs off the data protection assessment before any pilot with real applicants.
  • Privacy notice: applicants are told, in plain language and in their own language, what is used, who can see it, how long it is kept, how to delete it, and the risks of using a shared phone.

11. Development and implementation phases

I am building inFORMed in six phases. Each phase has a clear purpose, a set of activities, defined outputs and a point at which it is complete. Later phases depend on the approvals from earlier ones, so legal accuracy is built in from the start rather than checked at the end.

  1. Legal process discoveryI work with GRIT's legal advisor to map the protection order process, including exceptions, supporting documents and what happens after an application is prepared.

    Purpose

    To understand the full legal journey from the applicant's point of view before deciding exactly what the system does. The process does not end when the form is completed: the applicant still has to have it commissioned, see the magistrate, have it served on the respondent and return to court for the final order.

    Activities

    • Working sessions with GRIT's legal advisor to walk through the process step by step, from the first decision to apply to the final order and what happens if the order is breached.
    • Mapping the different pathways: applying for oneself, a helper applying on someone's behalf, applications involving children, urgent and after-hours applications, and situations where there is no domestic relationship and another process applies.
    • Identifying the points where the applicant needs information, the points where the clerk or magistrate decides, and the points where a referral is needed.
    • Listing every document involved at each step: the application, the applicant's confidential details, supporting affidavits, the interim order, the notice of the return date, the final order and the warrant of arrest.
    • Reviewing the existing inFORMed prototype and GRIT's current guides against the mapped process, to see what is already covered and what is missing.
    • Confirming the details of the agreed scope (section 12) with the legal advisor, so that every step of the process is covered.

    Outputs

    • A process map of the protection order journey, including exceptions and what happens after submission.
    • A list of decision points, branches and referral points.
    • Answers to the open questions in section 12.1, each with an owner and a date.

    Who is involved

    GRIT's legal advisor, myself, the safeguarding lead, and where possible a paralegal or court support worker who sees applicants in practice.

    Complete when: the legal advisor has reviewed and agreed the process map.

    • Confirming that each source is the current version and recording its effective date.
    • Extracting the relevant sections and writing an approved plain-language explanation for each.
    • Entering every source as a record with the fields in section 6, starting with the status "draft".
    • Legal review and approval of each record, with the reviewer and review date recorded.
    • Writing evaluation cases with the legal advisor: realistic but fictional scenarios with the expected result, including difficult and unusual cases. Real applicants' cases are never used.

    Outputs

    • A legal knowledge repository with every source recorded, reviewed and approved.
    • Approved plain-language explanations and a glossary.
    • A first set of evaluation cases.

    Who is involved

    The legal advisor (sourcing and approval), myself (structure and records), the Tech Lead (data entry and checks), native-speaker reviewers (translations) and the safeguarding lead (safety content).

    Complete when: every source the first module relies on is recorded with the status "approved", and none is still "draft" or "under review".

  3. Workflow and architecture designI design the questionnaire, branching logic, data model, legal rules, document-generation process and integration points.

    Purpose

    To turn the approved process map and sources into a precise design that I can build and test, and that the legal advisor can read and approve without reading code.

    Activities

    • Writing every question in plain language, with an explanation of why it is asked, in each supported language.
    • Defining for each question whether it is required or optional, what counts as a valid answer, and what happens if the applicant does not know or does not want to answer.
    • Defining the branches, for example when the applicant is applying for someone else, when children are involved, or when the matter is urgent.
    • Writing the legal rules, each linked to the source it comes from.
    • Mapping every answer to the exact box on the official form, including tick boxes.
    • Designing the data model for case information, with the minimum fields the form requires and nothing more.
    • Defining where AI is used and where it is not, following section 9, and how the applicant reviews any AI-assisted wording.
    • Defining the integration points: the website, WhatsApp, the AI services, document generation, and the safety and support services.
    • Completing the data protection assessment of the design with the Information Officer.

    Outputs

    • The workflow definition, in a structured, readable format.
    • The legal rules, each linked to an approved source.
    • The field mapping to Form 6 and Form 6A.
    • The case data model and the data protection assessment.
    • The technical architecture, including hosting, security and integration points (section 3).

    Who is involved

    Myself (design), the legal advisor (rules and questions), the Information Officer (data protection), the safeguarding lead (sensitive questions and safety pathways) and native-speaker reviewers (questions in each language).

    Complete when: the legal advisor has approved the workflow, rules and field mapping, and the Information Officer has signed off the data protection assessment.

  4. Build the first legal moduleI implement the protection order workflow, connect it to the legal knowledge service and build document generation.

    Purpose

    To build the protection order module on the shared platform, so that later legal modules reuse the same services.

    Activities

    • Building the shared platform services: safety checks, languages, voice input and listening, channels, consent and usage counters.
    • Implementing the workflow engine to run the approved workflow definition.
    • Connecting the legal knowledge service so that only approved records are used, with source references.
    • Building document generation on the official fillable form, with completeness checks and a review step for the applicant.
    • Adding the "Things to bring to court" checklist and the "what happens next" information.
    • Implementing the privacy and security controls in section 10.
    • Deploying to GRIT's own Google Cloud account in Johannesburg, with keys held in Secret Manager outside the code.
    • Building automated tests that run after every change, so that changes to the workflow or the AI model cannot silently break the module.

    Outputs

    • A working protection order module in a test environment.
    • Generated documents that match the official forms.
    • Automated tests and technical documentation.

    Who is involved

    Myself as AI Lead and developer, with support from the Tech Lead.

    Complete when: the full workflow can be completed end to end in the test environment in every supported language, and produces a correct, complete official form.

  5. Testing and legal validationI test standard cases, unusual circumstances, missing information, applicants acting on behalf of others, child-related scenarios and contradictory answers.

    Purpose

    To show, before any real applicant uses the module, that it is accurate, complete, safe and understandable.

    What is tested

    • Standard cases: typical applications completed correctly from start to finish.
    • Unusual circumstances: uncommon relationships, several respondents or incidents, applicants without identity documents.
    • Missing information: applicants who do not know dates, addresses or the respondent's details.
    • Applicants acting on behalf of others: the consent and capacity requirements are applied correctly.
    • Child-related scenarios: children affected, and applications involving maintenance or contact.
    • Contradictory answers: the system notices contradictions and asks the applicant rather than choosing for them.
    • Safety: danger, self-harm and distress are always met with the approved safety responses.
    • Language: conversations in each supported language, mixed languages and spoken input, with the form completed faithfully in English.
    • Faithfulness: the generated documents contain only what the applicant said, with no facts added, softened or strengthened.
    • Privacy and security: data is handled as designed, and nothing is stored or shared that should not be.
    • Usability: testing with people who represent the intended users, including people with limited reading ability, supported by a counsellor.

    Outputs

    • A test report, with every issue found and how it was resolved.
    • The legal advisor's sign-off of the generated documents and explanations.
    • The safeguarding lead's sign-off of the safety responses.

    Who is involved

    The legal advisor, the safeguarding lead, native-speaker reviewers, myself and the Tech Lead, and user testers supported by a counsellor.

    Complete when: all agreed tests pass, no serious issue remains open, and the legal advisor and safeguarding lead have signed off.

  6. Controlled deploymentI pilot inFORMed with appropriate users and legal support, review performance and errors, and expand only after the required approvals.

    Purpose

    To learn from real use in a safe, supported setting before the module is offered widely.

    Activities

    • Running a pilot with partner organisations, such as paralegals, shelters and court support services, so that pilot users have a trained person available to help them.
    • Monitoring the content-free usage counters: how many applications are started and completed, which languages and channels are used, and where people stop.
    • Collecting feedback from pilot users and the people supporting them.
    • Reviewing errors and near-misses regularly with the legal advisor and safeguarding lead, and correcting them.
    • Re-running the evaluation tests after every change before it reaches pilot users.
    • Agreeing the criteria for wider release, and using what the pilot shows to choose the next legal module.

    Outputs

    • A pilot report covering use, errors, feedback and changes made.
    • A decision on wider release, approved by GRIT's leadership.
    • The choice of the next legal module.

    Who is involved

    Partner organisations and their staff, the legal advisor, the safeguarding lead, myself and the Tech Lead, and GRIT's leadership for the release decision.

    Complete when: the pilot has met the agreed criteria and GRIT's leadership has approved wider release.

12.1 Open questions

These questions are still open. I will resolve each one with an owner and a date, and the second column records how the answer affects the design.

QuestionWhy it matters
1. Is GRIT comfortable with applicants' messages being processed by an AI service hosted outside South Africa, under an agreement with the provider?This is the most important data protection decision. If not, the design must remove names and identifying details before AI processing, or use a different provider.
2. Who is GRIT's Information Officer, and when can the data protection assessment be signed off?No pilot with real applicants can start without this sign-off.
3. Will trained support workers be able to help applicants complete or review their application?If yes, I add a way to share an application with consent, with secure storage, access control and audit logs. If no, inFORMed stays fully on the applicant's device.
4. Will the first version support people applying on behalf of someone else, such as a child or a person with a disability?This adds the consent and capacity requirements of Form 6 section 2 to the workflow and testing.
5. How does inFORMed respond when the applicant appears to be under 18?Children's personal information is specially protected, and the process, wording and referrals may differ.
6. What does inFORMed say about opening a criminal case alongside the protection order?Applicants often ask. The answer is approved general information and referral, not advice about their own case.
7. What does inFORMed say about urgent and after-hours situations?The guidance must be accurate for courts and police stations, and consistent with GRIT's safety responses.
8. Which partner organisations will support the pilot, and in which areas?Pilot users need a trained person nearby, and the court and service directory covers those areas first.
9. How often is legal content reviewed, and who watches for changes to the law or the forms?Sets the review dates in the legal knowledge records, and prevents the platform from relying on outdated sources.
10. What will GRIT measure to show that inFORMed works?For example, applications completed, languages used and where people stop. Measuring outcomes such as orders granted needs applicants' consent to follow up, which changes the data design.

13. Design principles

Legal accuracy first

Current, authoritative sources, with legal approval for every rule, explanation and document template.

Structured before generative

Explicit workflows and validation for every legally significant step. AI supports these controls and never replaces them.

Applicant in control

Applicants review, correct and approve all information and documents prepared for them.

Privacy by design

Case information is kept apart from legal knowledge, stays on the applicant's device, and is handled under strict controls.

Modular and extensible

Shared platform services and independent legal modules that are added and maintained separately.

Traceable and testable

Every piece of legal content and every rule is linked to its source, version, approval and tests.

15. Glossary

Protection order
A court order that prohibits a person from committing domestic violence and related acts. An interim order can be granted quickly; a final order is decided on the return date.
Applicant and respondent
The applicant is the person asking for protection. The respondent is the person the order is made against.
Interim order and return date
A temporary order granted before the respondent is heard, and the later court date on which the magistrate decides whether to make the order final.
Workflow engine
The part of the platform that decides which questions are asked, in what order, and which are required.
Legal rules engine
The part of the platform that applies approved legal and procedural rules, each linked to its source.
Legal knowledge service
The repository of approved, versioned legal sources and explanations the platform uses.
RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation: searching approved content for the relevant passages and giving them to the AI, so that answers come from verified sources with references.
Golden and red-team tests
Fixed test conversations with expected behaviour (golden), and deliberately difficult or risky ones (red-team), run after every batch of changes.
Evaluation data
Fictional test cases with expected results, used to measure accuracy, completeness and safety. Never real applicants' cases.
Cloud Run
Google Cloud's service for running a web server without managing machines. inFORMed runs on it in Johannesburg.
Secret Manager
Google Cloud's secure store for keys and passwords, so they are never written in the code.
Special personal information
Information that POPIA protects more strictly, including information about health and alleged criminal behaviour.
Information Officer
The person responsible for an organisation's compliance with POPIA.
